# Codex Legend — approved Facebook story style

Approved by Raphael on 2026-09-10. Reference: The Price of the Third Victory.
Tell the story of the pictured card, not Raphael's life, business or development process.

- Mostly natural spoken Tagalog, with a little English where it fits. Keep the exact English card title.
- Start with a specific curiosity, conflict, reversal or unanswered question from the story.
- Establish the scene with verified concrete details, then unfold events one moment at a time.
- Show the struggle before the turn. Earn any lesson through the story; do not force motivational advice onto tragedy or folklore.
- Pay off the opening. End with one natural story-related question or invitation to the next chapter, before the source note.
- Short spoken lines, frequent blank lines, natural pauses. No essay paragraphs, lists, hashtags or sales pitch.
- Vary openings and structure. Do not start every post with Did you know or copy the reference wording.
- Include the exact card name and set naturally near the end. The published post must include that actual card image.
- Research the specific story. No invented dialogue, events, thoughts, achievements, testimonials or cultural claims.
- Folklore is a telling, not historical fact. Say 'Sa isang bersyon...' when appropriate. Identify sources in a brief Tagalog note with direct URLs.
- Distinguish game art/flavor text from documented folklore. Never present a fictional card character as an attested cultural figure. Skip when evidence does not support the episode.
- Respect living communities and differences between traditions; avoid stereotypes and unsupported pan-Philippine generalizations.

The script's structural checks are safeguards, not proof of factual accuracy. Research quality still depends on the retrieved sources and model interpretation.
